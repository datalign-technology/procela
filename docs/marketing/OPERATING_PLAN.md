# Procela — Operating Plan (solo founder, seed-funded)

*The calibrated version. `GTM_PLAN.md` describes the motion; this describes
what one person with seed funding and 12–18 months of runway should actually
do, in what order, and — more importantly — what to **not** do.*

**Company reality this is built for:**

- **One person** selling *and* delivering. Mixed founding background with some
  industry credibility, but no dedicated delivery team.
- **Seed funded, 12–18 months of runway.** Free pilots are affordable. Time
  is not.
- **Relevant contacts in the ICP, but no conversations started.** The first
  twenty emails are warm.

---

## 1. The three constraints that reshape everything

### Constraint 1 — Delivery capacity is ONE pilot, not three

`DESIGN_PARTNER_PROGRAM.md` describes ten slots and cohorts of three. **That
is wrong for this company.** A 90-day pilot consumes roughly 6–8 hours a week
of founder time: the weekly working session, the deployment, the onboarding
sessions, the day-45 review, the ad-hoc support, the readout. Two concurrent
pilots is 15 hours a week, which is most of your selling capacity. Three is
all of it, and then you have three pilots and an empty pipeline.

**Run pilots sequentially, overlapping only at the tails.** Start pilot 2
around day 60 of pilot 1, when pilot 1 has moved from build to govern and
needs less of you.

> **Revised slot count: four, over twelve months.** Say "four founding
> partners" publicly. It is true, and four is scarce in a way that ten
> isn't — ten slots from a one-person company reads as a company that hasn't
> thought about delivery.

### Constraint 2 — Warm beats cold by an order of magnitude, and you have warm

You have contacts in the ICP you haven't pitched. That is the single most
valuable asset in this plan and it is currently unused.

| | Warm (known contact) | Cold (sequenced) |
|---|---|---|
| Reply rate | 50–70% | 3–6% |
| Positive reply | 25–40% | 1–2% |
| Emails to one pilot conversation | ~15 | ~400 |
| Founder hours per reply | Minutes | Hours |

**A solo founder should not lead with cold email.** Cold generates reply
volume you can't service and burns the months you need. The right sequencing
is: **warm first, associations second, cold third — cold as a background
process that fills the pipeline behind the warm list, not as the opener.**

Start the domain warmup in week 1 anyway (it takes 21 days and costs
nothing), but the first pilot will come from someone you already know, or
from someone they introduce you to.

### Constraint 3 — The runway clock sets the deadline, working backwards

Seed funding with 12–18 months from **September 2026** means the money runs
to roughly **September 2027 – March 2028**. Series A conversations have to
start 4–6 months before the end, so the evidence must exist by around
**June–September 2027**.

What a Series A needs from this company: **three referenceable customers, at
least two converted to paid, and the A1 milestone closed** — the first real
connector run against a live customer database, which is the open P0 that
validates the entire Phase-3 thesis.

Working backwards from June 2027:

| By | Must be true |
|---|---|
| **Dec 2026** (month 3) | Pilot 1 signed and kicked off |
| **Mar 2027** (month 6) | Pilot 1 completed with metrics. Pilot 2 signed. **A1 closed.** |
| **Jun 2027** (month 9) | Pilot 1 converted to paid. Pilot 2 completed. Pilot 3 signed. Two case studies published |
| **Sep 2027** (month 12) | 3 references, ~$80–120k ARR, Series A materials built on real evidence |

**The binding deadline is pilot 1 signed by end of December 2026.** Miss it
and every subsequent date slips past the fundraise. That single date is what
the next 90 days is for — not six to ten pilots, not a content engine, not a
brand.

## 2. Revised targets

The numbers in `GTM_PLAN.md` § 5 assume a team. These are the solo versions.

### 90 days (to 15 Dec 2026)

| Metric | Team plan | **Solo plan** |
|---|---|---|
| Unique contacts reached | 1,500 | **250** (40 warm + 210 cold) |
| Discovery calls | 45 | **20** |
| Tailored demos | 25 | **10** |
| Pilot proposals issued | 12 | **4** |
| **Pilots signed** | 6–10 | **1** (2 is an excellent quarter) |
| Case studies | 2 | **0** — none exist yet by definition |

### 12 months (to Sep 2027)

| Metric | Target |
|---|---|
| Pilots delivered | 3 |
| Converted to paid | 2 |
| ARR | $80–120k |
| Case studies | 2 |
| Reference calls available | 3 |
| A1 (real-customer connector run) | Closed |

**One signed pilot in 90 days is a good quarter for a solo founder.** Judge
yourself against that, not against the team plan's six.

### The revised solo funnel

```
40 warm contacts emailed personally (weeks 1-3)
  → 60%  reply                              ≈ 24 replies
  → 35%  of replies take a call             ≈ 8 discovery calls
  → 60%  advance to a tailored demo         ≈ 5 demos
  → 40%  advance to pilot conversation      ≈ 2 proposals
  → 50%  close                              ≈ 1 pilot          ← the first one
                                                                 comes from here

200 cold contacts (weeks 4-12, running behind the warm work)
  → 6%   reply                              ≈ 12 replies
  → 35%  positive                           ≈ 4 positive
  → 3 discovery calls → 2 demos → 1 proposal → pipeline for Q2
```

Cold email's job in the first 90 days is **to fill quarter two**, not to
produce the first pilot. Judge it on pipeline created, not pilots closed.

## 3. Revised industry priority

`GTM_PLAN.md` ranks by market attractiveness. A solo founder with a runway
clock must also rank by **sales-cycle length**, and that changes the order.

| Rank | Segment | Why it moves | Cycle |
|---|---|---|---|
| **1** | **Defense & shipbuilding — tier 2/3 suppliers, not primes** | CMMC 2.0 is a dated, contractual forcing function: no certification, no contract. 200–2,000 person companies where an owner or VP decides. Momentum Industries is already a seeded fixture | 60–90 days |
| **2** | **Investor-owned utilities — at division level, not enterprise** | Real budget, real regulatory pressure. Sell to a *department* — the Distribution or Customer Operations director — not to the enterprise | 90–150 days |
| **3** | **Mid-market manufacturing** | Private ownership, ERP-migration trigger, fast decisions, minimal procurement theatre | 60–120 days |
| **4** | **Healthcare — a single system's governance office** | Real pain, but committee-driven | 120–180 days |
| **5** | **State & local government** | *Deprioritised for direct selling.* Procurement is 6–18 months and unsolicited vendor contact can disqualify you from bidding. **Work it through associations only** — NASCIO, GFOA — as slow-burn relationship building. It is a year-two revenue line, not a year-one one | 180–540 days |

**The tactic that matters most: sell below the signature threshold.** Most
mid-market organisations let a director or VP approve $25–50k without
triggering procurement or an RFP. The $36k Governance Starter tier is
deliberately underneath that line at most of them. **Find the budget holder
who can sign without asking permission.** For a solo founder, this single
qualifying question is worth more than any campaign: *"If this works, who
signs, and what's the number above which it goes to procurement?"* Ask it on
the first call.

## 4. Charge for the pilot — a small fee, for a reason that isn't revenue

`DESIGN_PARTNER_PROGRAM.md` sets the pilot at $0. **Revise that: charge a
$12,000 implementation fee, licence free.**

Not for the money — seed funding means you don't need $12k. For **qualification**.

When you can only run one pilot at a time, picking the wrong one costs a
quarter you cannot get back. A free pilot attracts people who are curious.
A paid one attracts people with a budget, a sponsor and a deadline — and the
fee is the cheapest filter available for exactly the signal you need.

It also does three other things:

- **It sets a non-zero anchor.** Converting $0 → $39,000 is a harder
  conversation than converting $12,000 → $39,000.
- **It creates internal commitment.** A sponsor who defended a $12k spend
  shows up to the weekly session. One who got it free does not.
- **It is genuinely fair.** You are doing the deployment, the data loading
  and two onboarding sessions. That is real delivery work.

**How to frame it:**

> "The software licence is free for the ninety days — you'd be a founding
> partner and that's the deal. There's a $12,000 implementation fee that
> covers deployment into your environment, loading your data, and the
> onboarding sessions. If we hit the success criteria we agreed, that fee
> credits in full against year one."

Credit it against year one. It costs you nothing and removes the objection
entirely.

**Waive it in exactly one case:** a marquee logo whose reference value
exceeds the qualification risk — a name every other prospect in the segment
will recognise. Make that a deliberate decision, not a discount you slid into
under pressure.

## 5. The 90-day plan, week by week

### Weeks 1–2 — Warm list and infrastructure, in parallel

**The warm work is the priority. The infrastructure runs behind it.**

| Day | Action |
|---|---|
| Mon w1 | **Write the warm list.** Every person in the ICP you have a real relationship with — former colleagues, ex-clients, people who'd take your call. Target 40 names. Include the ones you think are a stretch |
| Mon w1 | Buy the sending domain, set SPF/DKIM/DMARC, start warmup (background; it takes 21 days) |
| Tue w1 | **Send the first 10 warm emails.** By hand. Sequence 0 in `EMAIL_CAMPAIGNS.md`. Ten, not forty — you need to hear the replies before sending the rest |
| Wed–Fri w1 | Stand up the hosted demo tenant (`PILOT_GO_LIVE_WORKSHEET.md` fast path). Brief counsel on ToS / privacy / DPA / pilot agreement |
| Mon w2 | **Send the next 15 warm emails**, revised from what the first 10 taught you |
| Tue–Wed w2 | Record the 6-minute async demo (`DEMO_PROGRAM.md` Format 0) |
| Thu–Fri w2 | **Take every call that came back.** This is the week's real work |
| Fri w2 | **Send the last 15 warm emails.** Warm list now exhausted — this is fine and expected |

**Week 2 gate:** ≥8 warm replies, ≥3 calls booked. If the warm list produced
fewer than 4 replies, the problem is the email, not the list — rewrite before
sending more.

### Weeks 3–6 — Convert the warm pipeline

| Week | Action |
|---|---|
| 3 | Discovery calls and tailored demos from warm replies. **Ask every single person, including the no's: "who else should I be talking to?"** Referrals out of a warm list are the second-best channel you have |
| 4 | First Gap Teardown sessions (`DEMO_PROGRAM.md` Format 3). Legal docs finalised. Cold sequencer goes live at 20/day — low volume, background |
| 5 | **First pilot proposal issued.** Pitch three association webinars (NDIA chapter, an EEI or AGA committee, a regional DAMA chapter) |
| 6 | Second-order referrals worked. Cold list built to 200 rows with triggers |

**Week 6 gate:** ≥1 pilot proposal outstanding with a named sponsor and an
agreed success-criteria draft. If not, you are not going to sign by December
— escalate: go back to your five strongest contacts and ask directly for the
pilot rather than for a meeting.

### Weeks 7–12 — Close one, and build quarter two

| Week | Action |
|---|---|
| 7 | Negotiate pilot 1. Do **not** start a second pilot conversation past proposal stage until 1 is signed |
| 8 | **Target: pilot 1 signed.** Kickoff scheduled inside 2 weeks — momentum dies in the gap between signature and kickoff |
| 9–10 | Pilot 1 kickoff and Define phase. Selling drops to ~40% of your time. This is expected and correct |
| 11 | First public "Procela in 20 Minutes" — now that you have a live customer story to reference, even anonymised |
| 12 | Quarter close. Pilot 1 in Connect phase. Pilot 2 conversation opened. Q2 pipeline: 200 cold contacts worked, 1–2 proposals live |

## 6. What to cut

More important than what to do. The team plan contains things that are
correct for a company with five people and fatal for a company with one.

| Cut | Why | Do instead |
|---|---|---|
| **High-volume cold email** (600/mo) | Generates replies you can't service and burns weeks | 200/mo, background, behind the warm work |
| **Biweekly public demo from week 5** | A recurring demo with three attendees damages credibility, and preparation is an hour a session | Start it at week 11, when you have a customer story |
| **LinkedIn 3×/week** | Real, but slow-compounding. Months to matter, and you have three | 1×/week. Post artifacts only — a generated hierarchy, a gap report. Zero effort, some signal |
| **Eight industry template packs** | Eight PDFs is a week of work for a lead magnet with no audience yet | Generate **two** — defense supplier and utility. Generate the others on demand, per prospect. It takes ninety seconds |
| **Partner channel** | Partners need enablement, collateral and a proven product. You have none of those yet | Revisit at month 6, after one pilot proves it works |
| **State & local direct selling** | 6–18 month procurement you cannot wait out | Association relationships only, as year-two seeding |
| **Conference sponsorship** | $25k and a quarter of preparation | Attend one, cheaply, with 15 pre-booked meetings |
| **A CRM with process** | Overhead before you have volume | A spreadsheet until 30 live opportunities. Then HubSpot Starter |

**The discipline:** for the next 90 days, if an activity does not plausibly
produce a signed pilot by December, it does not get founder hours. Brand,
content and community are correct investments — for month six.

## 7. The first hire, and when

You are the constraint. With seed funding you can afford to fix that, and the
timing matters more than the choice.

**Hire when pilot 1 reaches day 45** — proven demand, a real delivery load,
and enough evidence to attract someone good. Not before (nothing to hire
against), not after (you will be underwater and hiring badly).

**Hire the half you are worse at.** Be honest about which that is.

| If you are stronger at… | Hire | They own |
|---|---|---|
| Selling | **Solutions / delivery engineer** | Pilot delivery, deployments, onboarding, the connector install. Frees ~8 hrs/week of selling time and closes A1 properly |
| Building | **Founding AE with industry background** | Outbound, discovery, demos. Hire from the industry, not from SaaS — a former utility or defense-supplier operator who can sell beats a career AE who needs 6 months of domain education |

**Do not hire an SDR first.** An SDR generates meetings you don't have time
to take and pilots you can't deliver. The constraint is delivery capacity and
domain credibility, not meeting volume.

**Interim option, worth considering now:** two or three **advisors** — a
former utility CDO, a defense-supplier compliance lead — at 0.1–0.25% equity
each for monthly time and, crucially, introductions. Cheap, fast, and it
directly fixes the "mixed background" gap: you borrow the buyer network you
don't have. Recruit these in month 1, not month 6.

## 8. The solo founder's weekly rhythm

Protect the calendar or selling will lose to delivery every week, because
delivery has a customer asking and selling doesn't.

| | Focus | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Mon** | Selling. Outreach, follow-ups, the weekly scorecard | Never a delivery day. Start the week in the pipeline |
| **Tue** | Calls. Discovery, demos, teardowns | Batch them. Context-switching is the hidden tax on a solo founder |
| **Wed** | Delivery. Pilot working session + pilot work | The one fixed customer commitment |
| **Thu** | Calls + proposals | |
| **Fri** | Product, or the thing only you can do | Protect it. If it's always the first thing cut, the product stops improving and the pitch goes stale |

**The two rules that hold it together:**

1. **Monday is never a delivery day.** The week that starts in delivery ends
   with no pipeline, and you won't notice for three weeks.
2. **Four hours a week minimum on outreach, even mid-pilot.** The gap between
   pilot 1 finishing and pilot 2 starting is the most dangerous month in this
   entire plan, and it is created ten weeks earlier by a founder who stopped
   selling to deliver.

## 9. How to know it's working — and when to change course

### Month 1 — are people responding?

- ✅ ≥50% of warm contacts replied, ≥5 discovery calls held
- ⚠️ <30% warm reply rate → the email is wrong, or these contacts are less
  warm than you assumed. Call five of them instead of emailing
- 🚨 Nobody engages with the problem statement → the positioning is wrong,
  not the copy. Stop sending and go do ten problem-only interviews with no
  product in them at all

### Month 2 — are people buying the premise?

- ✅ ≥1 proposal out with a named sponsor and agreed success criteria
- ⚠️ Good demos, no proposals → you're reaching influencers, not budget
  holders. Change the targeting, not the demo
- 🚨 "Interesting, not now" universally → there's no forcing function in
  these accounts. Re-target on trigger events only (CMMC dates, rate cases,
  ERP migrations, audit findings)

### Month 3 — is one signed?

- ✅ Pilot 1 signed and kicked off
- ⚠️ Proposal stalled in procurement → you went above the signature
  threshold. Rescope to the $36k tier and find the director who can sign
- 🚨 No proposal converting → **this is the moment to consider a services-led
  entry**: sell a paid 4-week "Governance Gap Assessment" at $15–25k using
  Procela as the delivery tool. It's easier to buy, it's revenue, it gets
  you inside with real data, and roughly half of them convert to a platform
  deal afterwards. For a solo founder with runway, this is a legitimate
  path, not a retreat

### The signal that means stop and rethink

**Three well-run pilots that don't convert to paid.** Not a marketing
problem, not a pricing problem — a value problem. Pause outbound, go back to
the three sponsors, and ask what would have made them buy. Do it before the
runway forces the same conversation with less time to act on the answer.
