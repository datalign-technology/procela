# Procela — Go-to-Market Kit

Everything needed to generate interest, run demos, and convert the first
paying customers through a **founder-led, design-partner-first** motion.

> **Start with [`OPERATING_PLAN.md`](./OPERATING_PLAN.md).** The rest of the
> kit describes the motion at team scale. That document is the calibrated
> version for where the company actually is — one person selling *and*
> delivering, seed-funded with 12–18 months of runway, and a set of warm ICP
> contacts that have never been pitched. Where the two disagree, the
> operating plan wins.

This kit assumes what `docs/STATUS.md` says is true today: the product is
functionally near-complete (67 of ~80 capabilities Built), but it has never
run in production against a real customer, there is no multi-tenant SaaS
hosting, no billing subsystem, and the legal content is placeholder. That
shapes everything here. **We are not selling self-serve SaaS. We are
recruiting design partners into white-glove, single-tenant pilots** — four of
them over twelve months, run one at a time.

## The strategy in one paragraph

Every data-governance tool on the market starts at the data and asks the
business to catch up. Procela starts at the business process and lets the
data follow — and it is the only platform of the five benchmarked that can
generate a client's entire industry process hierarchy with AI in ninety
seconds, live, on a first call. That ninety seconds is the whole marketing
strategy: it is a demo moment no competitor can reproduce, it works on a
webinar, and it works in a screen-share with a stranger. So: email the forty
people you already know in the target industries a specific and falsifiable
promise, get them onto a 20-minute screen share, generate *their* hierarchy
in front of them, and close one of them into a 90-day Founding Partner pilot
— licence free, small implementation fee that credits against year one — in
exchange for a reference, a case study and roadmap access. Then do it again,
three more times, over the following year. Cold email runs quietly behind all
of that to fill the quarter after next.

## Start Monday — the first two weeks, in order

**Before any of this, read [`READINESS_GATES.md`](./READINESS_GATES.md).**
Two items there (real legal text, a hosted demo environment) genuinely block
outbound, and both are days of work, not weeks.

The ordering below is warm-first: the infrastructure runs in the background
while the warm list — the thing that will actually produce pilot one — gets
worked immediately. Week-by-week detail is in
[`OPERATING_PLAN.md`](./OPERATING_PLAN.md) § 5.

### Week 1

1. **Write the warm list. Monday morning, before anything else.** Every
   person in the ICP you have a real relationship with — former colleagues,
   ex-clients, anyone who'd take your call. Target 40 names, including the
   ones you think are a stretch. This list is your highest-value asset and
   it is currently unused.
2. **Send the first 10 warm emails, by hand**, from your own mailbox —
   Sequence 0 in [`EMAIL_CAMPAIGNS.md`](./EMAIL_CAMPAIGNS.md). Ten, not
   forty: read the replies before sending the rest.
3. **Buy a separate sending domain** and start automated warmup. It takes
   14–21 days and cannot be shortcut, which is why it starts now even though
   cold email doesn't begin until week 4.
4. **Stand up the hosted demo tenant** at a real URL, seeded with Tidewater
   Utilities and Momentum Industries. Never demo from a laptop.
5. **Brief counsel** on ToS, privacy policy, DPA and the pilot agreement.
   You cannot sign a utility or a defense supplier without them.

### Week 2

6. **Send the next 15 warm emails**, revised from what the first 10 taught
   you. Then the last 15 by Friday.
7. **Take every call that comes back.** This is the week's real work —
   protect the calendar for it.
8. **Record the 6-minute asynchronous demo**
   ([`DEMO_PROGRAM.md`](./DEMO_PROGRAM.md) Format 0). Every email links to it.
9. **Ask everyone, including the no's:** *"who else should I be talking
   to?"* Referrals out of a warm list are the second-best channel you have.
10. **Send the advisor email** (Sequence 0d) to your three or four
    best-connected industry contacts. Advisors buy you the buyer network the
    founding team doesn't have, and month 1 is when to recruit them — not
    month 6.

**Week-2 gate:** ≥8 warm replies and ≥3 calls booked. Fewer than 4 replies
means the email is wrong, not the list — rewrite before sending more.

## The one rule

**Do not sell more pilots than you can deliver.** For one person that ceiling
is **one at a time** — a pilot costs 6–8 hours a week, and two of them is
most of a selling week. Sell the next into a scheduled slot with an honest
start date; a waitlist converts better than an overloaded founder.

A pilot that goes badly with a utility will be known to every other utility
in the region within a quarter. This market talks. And with one pilot
running, there is no second one covering for it — your first is
simultaneously your reference, your case study and your Series A evidence.

## The one date

**Pilot 1 signed by 31 December 2026.** Working backwards from a seed runway
that ends in late 2027, that is the date every other date depends on. If the
warm list hasn't produced a proposal with a named sponsor by week 6, that is
the moment to escalate — go back to your five strongest contacts and ask
directly for the pilot rather than for a meeting.
